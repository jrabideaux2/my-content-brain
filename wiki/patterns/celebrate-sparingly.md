# Celebrate sparingly

**Definition:** Celebrations are reserved for meaningful customer accomplishments — not routine completions, not company milestones. Use a three-level framework to match emphasis to achievement.

**When to celebrate:**
- Goal achievement: debt payoff, savings target reached
- Major milestones: business anniversary, first tax filing completed
- Significant customer-valued outcomes
- Habit changes demonstrating progress
- Product-enabled promises fulfilled

**When NOT to celebrate:**
- Daily or routine completions (signing in, saving a file)
- Actions that imply typical reliability (saying "great job" for something that should always work implies it sometimes doesn't)
- Outcomes with potentially negative undertones (a tax deduction from a medical expense is not joyful)
- Any moment where it would feel condescending or tone-deaf

**Three levels (Intuit framework):**

### Level 1 — Small: routine recognition
- Encourages habit formation
- Minimal visual treatment
- Example: "Expense logged."

### Level 2 — Medium: key milestone
- Notable habit milestone, moderate emphasis
- Example: "You've reviewed expenses for 30 days straight."

### Level 3 — Large: major achievement
- Enhanced visuals, motion, more expressive copy
- Example: "You paid off your loan! Fantastic work."

**Example contrast:**
- ✓ "You paid off your loan! Fantastic work."
- ✗ "Right on! You've signed in successfully." — routine action, condescending

**Rules:**
- Check frequency — have they seen a celebration recently? Don't stack them.
- Consider proximity — don't celebrate something that precedes bad news
- Consider context — is the customer in an emotionally neutral place to receive it?
- Use Level 3 rarely — it loses impact if overused
