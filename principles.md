# Principles — Intuit Content Design System

Voice and content philosophy distilled from [contentdesign.intuit.com](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/). Channel-specific rules live in `wiki/voices/*`. This file holds what's true everywhere.

---

## Core voice principles (apply to every surface)

1. **Talk to your friends.** Write conversationally, like a knowledgeable friend — not a manual. Prioritize natural speech over strict grammar rules.
2. **Be clear and precise.** Use definitive language. Choose simple terms consistently to improve comprehension, accessibility, and translation accuracy.
3. **Speak to customers as "you".** Use "we/our/us" for the company; "you" for the customer. Use first person in CTAs when representing the customer's voice. Keep language gender-neutral; use "they/them" when needed.
4. **Think globally.** Keep sentences under 20 words. Avoid idioms, regional contractions, and complex metaphors. Use consistent terminology to support translation.
5. **Use active voice.** "We couldn't save your changes" beats "Your changes weren't saved." Reserve passive voice only for softening blame or errors.
6. **Use everyday contractions.** "We'll," "can't," "you're" — yes. "Ain't," "y'all" — no. Don't turn nouns into contractions.
7. **Use simple verb tenses.** Past, present, future simple only. Avoid progressive and perfect tenses unless conveying ongoing action.
8. **Get to the point.** Surface information only when users need it. Every word must earn its place.

## Brand personality (Intuit)

Five dimensions that shape all brand expression:

- **Forward thinking** — anticipates future solutions that enable customer success
- **Dynamic** — full of energy, challenges conventional approaches
- **Confident** — bold and assured, without arrogance
- **Authentic** — genuinely approachable and kind
- **Driven** — relentlessly fights for customers and their communities

## Content philosophy

- **Customer benefit, not company promotion.** Every screen asks: "What does the customer get from this?"
- **Reduce cognitive load.** One task at a time. One CTA per screen when possible. Surface info at the moment of need.
- **Test everything.** Internal preferences are irrelevant. Validate with actual users.
- **Accessibility is non-negotiable.** Aim for 5th–8th grade readability. Design for assistive technology from the start.
- **Think in moments.** Content is always experienced in context — someone's frustrated, in a hurry, or trying something for the first time.

## Cross-surface kill list

- "Oops," "Whoops," "Uh oh," "Yikes," "Hmm" — patronizing, especially when a user is stuck
- "Error," "invalid," "prohibited," "failed," "suspended," "fraud" — jargon that escalates anxiety
- "Please" in CTAs — passive; write the action directly
- "We're excited to..." / "Amazing" / "Awesome" — marketing voice doesn't belong in product UI
- "Click here" — vague; name the action
- "Sorry" unless Intuit's error caused meaningful customer effort — use sparingly
- ALL CAPS in body copy — reserved for marketing badges and table headers only
